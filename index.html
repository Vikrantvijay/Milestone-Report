Executive summary

There is enough text to build a useful next-word predictor, but the sources behave differently. Short social messages demand different handling from long-form news and blogs. A compact, source-balanced language model is the practical starting point.

03 / The prediction algorithm

Our product goal is straightforward: enter the beginning of a sentence and receive the three most likely next words. A statistical n-gram model is an efficient, explainable baseline for this task.

STEP 01Prepare text

Clean encoding, handle contractions, remove noise and retain high-value common words.

STEP 02Learn context

Count 1- to 4-word sequences; fall back to shorter matches for unseen phrases.

STEP 03Validate the model

Measure top-three accuracy and response time against a frequent-word baseline.

04 / From algorithm to Shiny

The first release will contain a single sentence-entry field, a Predict next word action and three ranked suggestions. No data-science knowledge will be needed to evaluate it. The acceptance criteria are fast response, reasonable prediction quality and a low-memory deployment.

Next decision: compare 3-gram and 4-gram accuracy on held-out text. Expand the training sample only if the improvement justifies the cost.
